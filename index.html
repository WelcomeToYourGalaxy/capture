Capture

Where the drug underworld sits inside governments and companies — 193 states scored, and the cases that reached a court
Map rundown−
There is no census of captured institutions. Nobody has counted them, and the reason is structural rather than clerical: UNODC's estimate is that around 0.2% of criminal proceeds laundered through the financial system are seized. What reaches a court is a small and non-random sample of what exists, biased toward the countries with independent prosecutors — which are the countries where capture is shallowest.

So this map does not claim a percentage. It carries layers that can each be checked, and keeps them apart.

The first is a 193-country score built from the Global Organized Crime Index 2025. Its state-embedded actors indicator measures officials, police and security personnel using state power for illicit advantage; 80 of 193 countries score 7.5 or higher, the band the Index calls severe. That indicator covers all organised crime, so on its own it is not a drug measure. It is combined here with all four drug-market indicators as a geometric mean, because capture by the drug underworld requires both a penetrable state and a drug economy large enough to be worth buying it. A country with severe state capture and no drug trade scores low, and so does a large drug economy inside a state nobody has managed to buy.

The second layer is the documented record: convictions, indictments, and regulatory settlements where an institution's capture was established or formally charged. A head of state convicted in New York. A cabinet minister who ran the federal police, convicted of taking cartel money. A navy chief taken in a sting. Roughly 30% of one national legislature investigated. A bank pleading guilty to conspiring to launder, with a branch manager paid in gift cards.

The distance between the layers is the finding. Twenty-two state cases and thirty-six firms, against fifty-one countries at severe exposure. The score estimates the exposure; the cases show what proof looks like when it exists, and how rarely it does.
